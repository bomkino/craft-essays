# Longer writing evaluation — v0.1.1

This follow-up tests longer writing with less direction than the [initial eight-case study](../README.md). It compares the published v0.1.0, v0.1.1 with two worked revisions, and writing produced without the skill.

The [three original briefs](cases.json) and [all nine responses, anonymous judgments, mappings and checks](comparison.json) are public. The material is synthetic; the people, places and events are invented for the evaluation.

## Change under test

Two annotated revisions add 422 words to the structure and expression references. One shows facts qualifying an argument; the other shows cadence and repeated action carrying hesitation. Both explain when another choice would serve the reader better. The 536-word core's instructions are unchanged. No test response was used as an installed example.

## Method

On 18 September 2026, a fresh-context agent wrote the briefs without seeing the skill, foundation or examples. Two supplied drafts of about 790 words ask only for improvement: a reflective personal essay and a civic argument. A third brief asks for a 700–900 word local-magazine feature from 249 words of notes.

Three fresh-context agents each completed all three briefs. One received only the briefs; one also received v0.1.0; one received v0.1.1. Both skill-assisted writers read the structure, substance and expression references. Writers did not receive the brief author's notes, expected answers, review history or other responses.

A fresh-context reader received the original briefs and independently shuffled anonymous A/B/C responses. It evaluated source fidelity, development, structure, voice and rhythm, with ties allowed. It could not see the skills or condition mappings. Conditions were revealed after its judgments were saved. The parent agent separately read every output and checked lengths. The saved outputs are unedited.

## Results

| Brief | Blind ranking | Main finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal essay | v0.1.1, then v0.1.0, then unassisted | The candidate makes a small revision that preserves self-awareness and keeps the ending unresolved. |
| Civic argument | v0.1.0, then v0.1.1 tied with unassisted | The prior version gives the most balanced, usable trial proposal. The candidate adds useful reasoning but assumes comparison data that the source has not established. |
| Magazine feature | v0.1.0, then v0.1.1, then unassisted | The prior version best balances invitation and restraint. The candidate has strong transitions and rhythm, but some conspicuous cleverness and unnecessary explanation. |

Against the previous version, the candidate was preferred once and lost twice. Against unassisted writing, it was preferred twice and tied once. These are one reader's judgments on three individual responses, not estimates of how often the skill improves writing.

All feature responses meet the length requirement: 701 body words unassisted, 728 with v0.1.0 and 761 with v0.1.1, using whitespace splitting and excluding titles. Factual checking still matters. Both the unassisted and candidate features call the performance an evening event, although the notes give no time of day. The candidate argument needs a prospective baseline or a narrower comparison: only historical arrivals are established, not historical unsuccessful requests and staff administration time. The unassisted argument also overstates the removal of telephone work despite nine weekly telephone bookings remaining.

## Decision and limits

Keep the two examples as concise demonstrations of difficult choices. The comparison supports no claim that adding them improves writing overall. It does show a useful personal-essay revision and makes the remaining weaknesses inspectable. One preference does not justify a new general rule, and no rules were added in response to these results.

The source essay was already accomplished; leaving successful passages alone was a legitimate result. The sparse feature notes created pressure to meet length through reflection, which all three responses sometimes stretched into repetition. A real publication could benefit more from additional reporting than further polishing.

This is one response per condition per brief, in English, with one agent reviewer. It is not a human taste study, a statistical comparison, a cross-model benchmark or proof across every writing form. Packaging, local discovery and Cloud installation are separate checks; Cloud writing execution was not exercised in this evaluation.
