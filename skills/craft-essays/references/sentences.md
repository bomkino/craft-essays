# Sentences

Line work for every medium: pass 7, or any time one sentence is the problem.

- **Let verbs carry the action.** Prefer the verb that shows it to is, has and there is: "the kettle shrieked" over "the kettle was loud".
- **Comparisons.** Name the thing first, then the quality it shares, then the comparison: "The pan came out black and crusted as a February road." Prefer metaphor to simile, one image reused to three that compete, and plain words when a comparison adds nothing. Drop "like" where the sentence allows it: "she breathed fast as a sprinter". Each comparison sends the reader out of the scene, so bring them straight back. An extended comparison can be the pleasure of the line when you commit to it.
- **Rhythm.** Vary length with the thought: short to land a turn, long when the sentence has somewhere to go. Give neighbouring paragraphs different shapes. Save the one-line paragraph for the single beat that most needs it.
- **Sound.** Hard consonants (P, K, T, D, B) carry and land like jabs; runs of soft S, F and V sounds slacken. Repeat a vowel or consonant on purpose in the lines that matter.
- **Attribution and gesture as punctuation.** In dialogue and quotation, "she says" and a small action set the length of a pause more precisely than commas.
- **Refrains and utility phrases.** A returning line marks time and lets a shock settle. A speaker's habitual phrase for when words fail can do the same.
- **Specific labels.** Where a pronoun or a general noun blurs the picture, use the label this speaker would use. Keep pronouns and stable terms wherever clarity depends on them: instructions, contracts, technical writing.
- **Units.** Measure time, distance and cost in the units the speaker lives by.
- **Placeholders.** In a draft, `??????` marks a missing fact, detail or beat. Keep writing, and fill it later with a particular you find; finished work leaves none.
