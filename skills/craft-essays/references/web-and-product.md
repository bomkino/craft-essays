# Web and product copy

Websites, landing pages, product and interface copy, campaigns, about pages, posts and newsletters. Visitors skim until something is specific enough to stop them. Keep the particular, its proof and the next step within reach of each other.

## Headlines

- **The headline is big voice; the line under it is little voice.** The headline makes one plain claim or observation about the visitor's situation. The next line says what the thing is, who it is for, and what it does or costs, in particulars. A headline that needs a paragraph to decode it is decoration.
- **Treat every label as a question.** This genre runs on received text: seamless, elevate, bespoke, world-class, bring your vision to life. Answer each with a fact from the material: what the product does, to what, in how long, for how much. When the material holds no answer, cut the sentence.
- **Measure in the visitor's units** when the facts support it: minutes of their day, the tool they already own, the step they dread.

## Proof

- **Head:** the process in specifics, the figures you actually have, the tools, the clients you may name, the thing only a practitioner would say.
- **Heart:** a candid limit, a price stated up front, the trade-off of a choice. One honest limit makes every other claim more believable.
- Keep proof within a scroll of the claim it supports. Testimonials, figures and logos are real and approved; where one is missing, the page works without it and the gap goes on the list for the author.

## Submerge the "we"

Company copy drifts toward describing its own passion. Keep the camera on the visitor's problem and on the work. Bring in "we" where it takes responsibility or makes a choice: what we read before we quote, what we decline, who answers the email. Care shows in the particulars of the work (the revisions, the files, the handover) more than in adjectives about caring.

## The page

- **One job per section:** orient, show, prove, answer or ask. Sections with the same job merge.
- **Horses down the page.** Carry two or three ideas from hero to footer; each section returns to one with new evidence. Twelve unrelated benefits make a brochure nobody remembers.
- **Textures.** Alternate prose with a list, a figure, a short FAQ, a real quotation, a price table.
- **Literal where it counts.** Prices, terms, eligibility, consent, forms, errors and navigation say exactly what they mean, in the exact wording the material gives.
- **Calls to action name what happens next.** "Book a 20-minute call" tells the visitor more than "Let's talk".

## Posts, announcements and newsletters

- **Announcements thumbnail:** what changed, when, and what the reader does about it, in the first two lines. List what stays the same as completely as what changes; readers check both against their own plans.
- **A newsletter feature can open on the idea** and arrive at the news, as long as it closes on the action: the link, the date, the next step.
- **One particular** that makes the item worth reading: the reason, the detail, the small absurdity. Then stop.
- A post that reminds readers of their own version of the story does more than one that asks them to comment.

## Rewriting generic copy

Mark every label. For each, find the fact in the material that replaces it; where there is none, cut. Meet a required length with more particulars from the material. Listen for the author in their notes (the aside, the plain boast, the thing they are proud of) and let the page sound like them. The fog goes; the author's true facts and best phrases stay.
