# Substance: give language something to carry

## Find the warrant

When writing asks for trust, identify what earns it: relevant knowledge, evidence, experience, reasoning or accountable observation. Put the necessary support close enough to the claim for its relation to be clear. Make a defensible judgment; preserve its limits.

Candour can establish the writer's position without performative confession. In an apology, “I missed the deadline” takes responsibility that “a delay occurred” conceals. In criticism, a particular observation gives the reader grounds to consider a judgment. Technical vocabulary helps when it is accurate and useful to the audience; its obscurity alone proves nothing.

Vivid particulars are not evidence merely because they sound convincing. Preserve the distinction between what happened, what the writer infers and what the writer imagines.

## Examine the shortcut

Find the important adjective, stock phrase or general claim that is doing too little. What action, observation, example, relation or consequence would make it intelligible or particular?

“A seamless content experience” gives a new user less than “Save keeps your work as a draft. Publish makes it visible to other people.” The latter's specificity lies in the relationship, not in sensory description.

Use the actual material. If the needed fact or experience is absent, obtain it, leave a clearly identified gap when a provisional draft is requested, or make a more modest claim. Inventing a testimonial, statistic, memory or motive is not a prose improvement.

A precise abstraction can compress a thought beautifully. A familiar expression can be socially right. Keep either when it works. Fresh attention matters more than unusual synonyms.

## Make experience consequential

When experience is central, select details that affect perception or action: the weight that changes a movement, the sound that interrupts a thought, the small convenience that makes a place comfortable. Carry relevant conditions through the passage; an established burden should not vanish between sentences.

Describe observable behaviour accurately. In reporting, a gesture does not by itself establish another person's private motive. Quiet sensation can matter as much as intensity. Choose the detail that contributes to this experience rather than running through every sense.

## Compare precisely

Before keeping an analogy, identify the exact shared property, action or relationship. Give the reader enough of the actual subject to understand the comparison. An explanatory analogy may need a limit; an expressive image needs aptness within this voice and piece.

Each new comparison asks the reader to enter another frame of reference. Prefer a coherent, developed image when several unrelated images scatter attention. Sometimes literal language is stronger. Sometimes lingering over a comparison is the pleasure the passage offers.

**Decision check:** What does the detail or comparison reveal? Is it supported or clearly imagined? Does the reader gain understanding or experience worth the attention it asks for?
