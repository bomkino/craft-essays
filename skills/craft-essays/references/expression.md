# Expression: voice, movement and pleasure

## Keep the person in the writing

Voice lives in what the writer notices, knows, values and chooses to say, as well as in vocabulary and syntax. Look for those commitments in the supplied material. Preserve a useful irregularity, an opinion with reasons, a characteristic turn or a meaningful hesitation.

For new writing, let the relationship and occasion set the register. A friend, critic, researcher and public speaker may need different degrees of intimacy, qualification or force. Compose with appropriate affection, wit, anger, curiosity or restraint from the beginning; personality need not be added as a closing flourish.

Keep the author's chosen names, pronouns and exact terms clear. Changing a reference can express perspective, but decorative renaming can confuse. First person is available for experience, argument, feeling and responsibility.

## Hear the thought unfold

Attend to sentence stress, cadence and the work a reader must hold in mind. Put weight where the meaning needs it. A short sentence can make a turn audible; a long one can sustain feeling or keep qualifications in relation. Vary length for the thought, not to meet a pattern.

Repetition can establish rhythm or let an image return with new meaning. A connective phrase, greeting or acknowledgment can provide orientation, ease or breathing space without introducing a fact. Test whether a cut preserves the passage's human and musical function.

For speech, make references and attributions clear to listeners who cannot look back. Leave room for a realization, laugh or pause. If a real read-through is available, use what it reveals. Silent inspection is useful editorial work; it is not audience testing.

## Change texture when something is missing

A stream of explanation may need an example. A list may need interpretation. A close description may need reflection or a little distance. A change to direct address may make the reader's place clear. Choose a different mode to do that work, then connect it to the whole.

Variation has a cost. A short note can succeed in one mode, and an interesting digression can still be wrong for its location. Formatting carries expectations: a faux report, quote or testimonial must not impersonate factual evidence.

## Let surprise arise from perception

Look for a particular mismatch: what a system promises and what it does, what an object once meant and what it means now, what a ritual requires and what anyone actually needs. That observation can generate humour or a serious insight.

For a playful sign about a jammed printer, “It has eaten three sheets and would like a fourth” develops the supplied situation. An unrelated joke would merely decorate it. In a serious note, plain acknowledgment may be the stronger choice.

Permit beauty, ambiguity, stillness and difficulty when they belong. A distinctive passage need not become frictionless professional copy. Judge how its form carries its meaning.

**Decision check:** Does this sound like the intended writer addressing this reader? Do rhythm, texture and surprise support that encounter? Which valuable quality would a smoother or shorter version lose?
